\abstract{%
\linespacing{1.2}
Machine learning models in production can lose accuracy without warning when the distribution of their input features drifts. Classifier-based detectors such as D3 compare recent data with a sliding reference, which can miss slow drift, use an uncalibrated threshold, which raises false alarms on autocorrelated streams, and do not say which features changed. This thesis studies adversarial validation, a classifier two-sample test (C2ST), as a technique for unsupervised feature drift detection and proposes the Explainable Adversarial Drift Detector (EADD). EADD compares the newest window with a reservoir sample of all data since the last alarm, confirms each alarm with a permutation test whose null distribution comes from earlier windows of the same stream, stops that test early with an adaptive sequential permutation test (ASPT), and ranks the drifting features with TreeSHAP. Four hypotheses were registered before the final runs and tested on fresh seeds over 35 streams: 21 with abrupt, gradual or incremental drift, eight without drift and six autocorrelated streams without drift. H1: on slow (gradual or incremental) drift, EADD detects more drift points (H1a) and detects them sooner (H1b) than D3 with the same LightGBM classifier (D3-LightGBM), and on abrupt drift its mean time to detection (MTD) is at most 50 samples higher (H1c). H2: on streams without drift, EADD raises fewer false alarms than D3. H3: ASPT gives the same recall, MTD and false alarms as the full permutation test (H3a) with fewer permutations (H3b). H4: the TreeSHAP attribution of a classifier-based drift detector ranks the truly drifting features above chance. All four were supported. On slow drift, EADD had a recall of 1.00 against 0.53 for D3-LightGBM (Holm $p = 0.015$) and an MTD of 443 against 879 samples (Holm $p = 0.023$); on abrupt drift its MTD was 115 against 122 samples (Holm $p = 0.023$). On autocorrelated streams without drift it raised 5.59 false alarms per 10{,}000 samples against 19.5 for D3-LightGBM and 18.5 for D3, fewer on all six streams (Holm $p = 0.023$ against D3-LightGBM, 0.016 against D3). ASPT gave equivalent recall, MTD and false alarms with 52 instead of 163 permutations per test. Measured with EADD, the TreeSHAP ranking reached a precision@k of 0.75 against 0.31 by chance ($p < 0.001$). The results support a reservoir reference with a history-based null for detecting slow feature drift with few false alarms, although on abrupt drift EADD is equivalent, not better.
}
