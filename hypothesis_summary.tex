{\large\textbf{EADD thesis: hypotheses and evidence}}\hfill Nusrat Begum, \today

\textbf{Setup.} Four INSECTS streams with annotated drift points (10 drift points), five seeds, so 20 runs per detector. No synthetic data. EADD and D3 use the same LightGBM classifier and the same windows ($N_{ref} = 500$, $N_{cur} = 200$, $\tau = 0.7$); only D3 with LightGBM (D3-LightGBM) is used. The six benchmark detectors of Lukats et al.\ (2024) (BNDM, CSDDM, IBDD, OCDD, SPLL, UDetect) were rerun with 500 observed samples; no published numbers were copied. A detection matches a drift point if it occurs within 500 samples after it. \textbf{MTD} averages the delay over all drift points and counts a missed one as 500 (Lukats et al.\ average only over found drifts, which favours a detector that finds one drift quickly and misses the rest). Tests: one-sided Mann--Whitney U (MW) on the 20 runs, with the paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test (W) as a check; $\alpha = 0.05$.

\textbf{Test:} recall 0.50 vs.\ 0.65, MW $p = 0.90$ (W $p = 0.96$); MTD 302 vs.\ 279, MW $p = 0.78$ (W $p = 0.85$). \textbf{H1 is not supported.} 16 of EADD's 21 missed drift points had an EADD detection in the 500 samples \emph{before} the annotated point; accepting those, both detectors reach recall 0.95. EADD checking every 200 samples (as D3 does): recall 0.80, MTD 204 (not significant, $p = 0.12$, $p = 0.075$).

\textbf{Conclusion.} With confounds removed, EADD does not beat D3-LightGBM on annotated real data (H1, H2 not supported); ASPT keeps the decisions of a full permutation test at lower cost (H3 supported) and is needed only at low thresholds.
